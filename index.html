The pricing model, plainly

What renting a network actually costs

Several enterprise platforms charge an annual subscription per switch and per access point for the right to manage hardware you already bought. Let the renewal lapse and the console degrades or locks. The equipment keeps drawing power in your closet and you lose the ability to change a password on it.

UniFi charges no recurring per-device license for switching, wireless, routing, cameras or door access. Buy the gear once and manage it for as long as it runs. That is a property of the platform, and it is most of the reason I standardized on it.

Why this matters more than a speed test

A congested 2.4 GHz band cannot be fixed with a better access point, because the interference is coming from outside the building. It gets fixed by moving clients to 5 and 6 GHz, narrowing channel width, and placing radios where measurement says they belong.

A console showing seven networks looks segmented. If every device still sits on one physical subnet, a thermostat can still reach the finance workstation. The console was telling the truth about its configuration and nothing about the wiring.

Scope

How the work is bounded

Every engagement is quoted as a fixed scope at a fixed price, phased so you can see what you are buying before the next phase starts. There is no monthly tier to grow into and no agreement that renews itself while nobody is watching.

01

Assess and advise

A survey of what you have, what is failing, and why. You get the findings in writing whether or not you hire me. This often concludes that you do not need the thing you were about to buy.

02

Design only

Full design package, addressing plan, channel and power plan, and a bill of materials. Your team or your existing contractor builds it. I stay reachable through the build.

03

Design and deploy

I design it, stage it, configure it, cut it over and document it. One engineer from the first walkthrough to final validation, so nothing is dropped in a handoff between people.

04

Support, optional

Monitoring, firmware lifecycle and configuration changes on what I built, scoped by sites, hours and severity. You keep the hardware and the documentation whether or not you take it.

Sectors

Sanctuaries, gyms, classrooms, baseyards

Most network design assumes a floor of desks with predictable density and a ceiling you can drill. A campus with a hard-ceilinged auditorium, a reflective gym, concrete between classrooms and cameras out at the property line behaves nothing like that, and the difference shows up during the first week of use.

  • Houses of worship five distinct coverage problems under one roof and a deadline every Sunday
  • Schools and education device density that arrives all at once, filtering obligations, and a refresh window the length of a summer
  • Nonprofits capital funding available, recurring software budget unavailable, which is the exact shape this platform fits
  • Public agencies documented segmentation and an audit trail, procured on a calendar that belongs to someone else
