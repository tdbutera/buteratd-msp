Sectors

Buildings that were never offices.

Every sector below shares one trait: density, geometry or obligation that a standard office design gets wrong on the first day of real use.

01

Houses of worship →

Five radio environments under one roof, a livestream competing with guest wireless for the same upstream, cameras near children's ministry, and a volunteer team who runs it on Sunday morning.

Deepest experience. Two active engagements.

02

Schools and education

Device density that arrives in a single bell, content filtering obligations, testing windows that cannot degrade, and a hardware refresh that has to start and finish inside a summer.

03

Nonprofits

Grant and capital funding available for equipment, recurring software budget unavailable or unpredictable. A platform with no per-device license fits that funding shape better than a subscription does.

04

Public agencies and baseyards

Documented segmentation, an audit trail, outdoor and yard coverage, and a procurement calendar that belongs to somebody else. Documentation is usually the deliverable that matters most.

05

Small and mid-size business

Warehouses, clinics, restaurants and multi-tenant offices. Typically one site, one closet, and a wireless problem that has been misdiagnosed twice.

06

Defense supply chain

Firms that have to show CMMC evidence. The burden is almost always documentation and segmentation rather than new equipment, which is the cheaper finding to act on.

What carries across all of them

  • Radio placement from measurement, because geometry and materials differ per room
  • Segmentation by role, so one compromised device does not reach the finance workstation
  • Upstream capacity planned before the thing that needs it is installed
  • Documentation good enough for your own staff, or the next engineer, to work from
