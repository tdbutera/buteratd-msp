The problem shape

Six problems, one building

A church campus is not a floor of desks, and the pieces interact. Fixing coverage without fixing upstream capacity moves the complaint from the lobby to the livestream.

RF

Five radio environments

A hard-ceilinged sanctuary holding a thousand phones. A lobby with concentrated foot traffic. A gym that reflects everything. Classrooms with concrete between them. A lot that still needs cameras. Each needs its own channel and power plan.

WAN

The congregation is the load

Guest wireless peaks at the same moment the stream needs upstream bandwidth. Share one uninspected uplink and the stream is what degrades, and the people who notice are the ones who are not in the room.

AV

Streaming fails on the network first

Dropped frames usually trace to upstream contention, a switch making its own decisions about multicast, or an encoder on wireless that should have been wired. Guaranteed upstream and a wired encoder path stop the AV team from debugging someone else's layer.

CAM

Cameras near children

Entrances, hallways, the lot, and the areas around children's ministry. Coverage, retention period and who may view footage are policy decisions with technical consequences. Recording on site keeps the video on your property.

OPS

Volunteers run it on Sunday

Whoever is in the building is who fixes it, and that person usually has a day job. A system that needs a specialist stays broken until the specialist is free. I design for the team you have and train them on their own equipment.

SEG

Check-in, giving, and the office

Children's check-in stations, giving kiosks and the finance workstation should not share a broadcast domain with guest wireless and a smart thermostat. Decide where that boundary falls early, and write it down.

Safeguarding

Coverage becomes a safety question

There is no per-camera fee, no per-access-point fee and no per-door fee on this platform. The question in the room changes from how many cameras the software budget allows to how much of the campus needs to be covered.

That is a property of the licensing model. It is not a discount I am offering, and I would rather you understand the difference before you sign anything.

  • Retention window is a storage decision you make, not a subscription tier
  • Footage stays on your property under your access policy
  • Door access and cameras share the console the wireless already uses

Sequencing

Nothing significant changes on a Saturday

Cutovers land midweek with the previous path still available. Saturday is for verification, and Sunday is for nothing at all. This is the single scheduling rule I will not negotiate, because a rollback needs daylight and a person who is awake.

  1. Walk every building

    Including the annex, the portable and the storage room with a switch in it.

  2. Survey before specifying

    Radio placement from measured signal and measured interference.

  3. Stage on the bench

    Configured and burned in off site, so install day is mounting and patching.

  4. Run parallel, then cut

    New alongside old where the building allows, verified before the old path is retired.

  5. Document as I go

    Diagrams, addressing, port assignments, channel plan, and credentials into a vault your leadership controls.

  6. Train the volunteers

    On your equipment, on a weeknight, until the Sunday team can do the ordinary things alone.

Bench staging, before install day
  • Firmware brought to the version the design names, on every unit
  • Adopted to your controller, named and addressed per the plan
  • Networks, wireless and switch profiles applied and checked against the design
  • Labeled front and back with the name the documentation uses
  • Powered for a burn-in before anything is carried into the building
  • Configuration backed up, so install day is mounting and patching

Multi-site

Four campuses should not mean four networks

Campuses, a school, an office and a baseyard managed as separate estates means four logins, four policies and four people who each understand one of them. One console applies the same segmentation everywhere and shows you all of it at once.
